# Slice 1.4 — Payout and refund compensation

*Merged 2026-11-09 · [PR #3](https://github.com/AdamDiouri/idem/pull/3) · [plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-11-09-slice-1.4-payout-and-compensation.md) · written retrospectively on 2026-11-10 from the PR and commit record*

This slice completes the saga: charge the customer, pay the merchant, and refund the customer if the payout definitely fails. The hard part is the payout, the pivot: once it may have paid the merchant, we can no longer safely undo the charge.

## What shipped

- **Payout step (the pivot).** It pays the merchant `amount − fee`. Only a *definite* payout failure triggers compensation. A payout still ambiguous when its attempts run out goes `DEAD`, and the workflow `NEEDS_ATTENTION`, because the merchant may already have been paid.
- **Refund step.** It returns the full charge, fee included. Success ends `COMPENSATED`; refusal ends `NEEDS_ATTENTION`. The charge it refunds is frozen in a typed `steps.refund_charge_ref` column when the refund is created, so every retry sends an identical request.
- **Compare-and-swap workflow transitions** on the expected current status.
- **Ledger postings** for payouts and refunds. A compensated workflow nets to zero on every account.
- **Fake provider** `/payouts` and `/refunds` on one shared idempotency engine, with per-resource keys, refund limits, a `payout_fail` fault and invalid merchant accounts.
- **New invariants.** I3: the ledger agrees with provider effects, amounts included, and every refund reverses a charge of its own workflow. I5: no payout without a charge. I7: each terminal status matches what moved.

## What broke or surprised us

An independent reviewer found no double payout, double refund, refund-and-payout or refund without a charge. It did find four Important issues, all fixed before merge:

- **A parked workflow could still move money with no ledger record.** Its steps were still claimable. The money moved, then the status check rolled back the completion and the step retried forever. Now only steps of active workflows are claimed, and a successful charge advances only a `RUNNING` workflow.
- **The invariants could not see a refund against another workflow's charge.** A new I3 rule checks refund ownership.
- **The pivot test covered only the case where no money moved.** A money-moved variant was added. That exposed that a parked workflow's ledger may legitimately lag the provider until a human reconciles it, so I3 now exempts `NEEDS_ATTENTION` workflows from the amount comparison and `idem check` lists them instead.
- **The 30-workflow fault test asserted too little.** An engine that parked everything would have passed. It now asserts the exact outcome mix.

## Evidence

- Flow tests: happy path (the ledger keeps exactly the fee); F20 payout refused, full refund, ledger nets to zero; F21 refund keeps failing, `DEAD`, `NEEDS_ATTENTION`; ambiguous payouts never refunded, with and without money moved; parked workflows move no money; a crash during payout.
- `TestFlow_ManyWorkflowsUnderFaults`: 30 concurrent workflows, 20% faults everywhere, exact outcome mix, zero violations.
- Every engine failure and flow test from slices 1.2 to 1.4 now ends by checking I1, I2, I3, I5 and I7.
- `make test` (engine package 3 times in a row), `make lint` 0 issues, `make build-release`.
- Compose smoke test: `SUCCEEDED`, `COMPENSATED` and `FAILED` workflows; a `SUCCEEDED` after an ambiguous payout attempt with exactly one payout; exactly two fees in the ledger; `idem check` 0 violations across 6 effects.

## Left open

Review minors deferred:
- `refund_charge_ref` is frozen by convention, not by a database trigger (done in 1.6).
- A payout with no successful charge retries instead of parking.
- I3 checks posting totals, not individual accounts.
- No alert for a step stuck beyond its attempt limit.
- Consider parking a payout that gets a permanent answer after an ambiguous attempt, rather than refunding. This became the pivot safety rule in 1.5.
