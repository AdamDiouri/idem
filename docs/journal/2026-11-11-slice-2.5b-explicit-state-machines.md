# Slice 2.5b — Explicit state machines

*Merged 2026-11-11 · [PR #18](https://github.com/AdamDiouri/idem/pull/18) · [plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-11-11-slice-2.5b-explicit-state-machines.md)*

A review of implicit state found the workflow and step statuses well constrained in their values but not in their moves: the allowed transitions existed only as scattered compare-and-swaps, the design doc had drifted from them, and four places decided "money may have moved" their own way. This slice writes the machines down once and has the database enforce them.

## What shipped

- **One definition of "money may have moved"**, `ClaimedStep.MoneyMayHaveMoved()`, now read by the pivot rule, the dead reasons, sticky doubt, the lookup near key retention and the hook-failure limit.
- **Workflow and step transitions enforced by the database** (migrations 00017, 00018, D23). A trigger refuses any move not in the table, with SQLSTATE `23514` naming the row and both statuses. "Terminal" and "active" are SQL functions every query reuses.
- **The outbox requeue checks the event's status in SQL**, like the step queries.
- **The fake provider's webhook deliveries** have one state (`queued`, `sending`, `delivered`, `gaveUp`) instead of three booleans.
- **Chaos retargets** a kill or pause whose worker or relay is mid-restart, so bad timing cannot cost a seed a whole event kind.
- **Design §8.2 and §8.3** are now the enforced tables, and a new §8.4 lists the states encoded in other columns.

## What broke or surprised us

- **The review's suspicion was right in code.** Given a step with an earlier ambiguous send whose status just before the claim was not `IN_DOUBT`, the "attempts exhausted, no effect" branch parked it as `attempts_exhausted_no_effect`: money certainly not moved, no page. The state seems unreachable today, but the test reproduced the wrong answer before the fix. Two more readers (sticky doubt, and choosing a lookup over a re-send near key retention) had the same inconsistency and now use the predicate.
- **Most test fixtures were already legal.** Of the hand-written status updates in tests, only a few broke under the triggers: fixtures that parked a `PENDING` workflow, or set a `PENDING` step straight to `DEAD`. They now go through legal paths, or through `testutil.Plant` (owner, triggers off) when only a fixture needs the state. Two tests changed meaning on purpose: the dead-reason schema test plants `RUNNING` first so it still tests its constraint, not the new trigger.
- **The same flake for relays.** The first campaign after the worker fix failed as vacuous: its only `kill_relay` found the relay restarting after a failpoint. Relays now retarget too.
- **My own slip:** I ran `make build-release` while campaigns ran, which replaced `bin/idem` with a binary without failpoints, and two campaigns could not start. Rebuilt and re-run.

## Evidence

- Tests: every edge of both tables allowed and every other move refused (49 workflow pairs, 49 step pairs), Go and SQL agree on terminal statuses, the predicate's matrix, the parking fix, retargeting for workers and relays.
- Chaos campaigns, seeds 1, 2 and 3 (90s): PASSED, 0 violations; 872, 897 and 884 outcomes delivered; no process logged a refused transition.
- `make test`, `make lint`, `make vuln`, `make build-release`.
