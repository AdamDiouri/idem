# Slice 2.1 — Async payouts and deadline polling

*Merged 2026-11-10 · [PR #6](https://github.com/AdamDiouri/idem/pull/6) · [plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-11-10-slice-2.1-async-payouts.md) · written retrospectively on 2026-11-10 from the PR and commit record*

Real payouts are asynchronous: the provider answers `202 pending` and the result comes later. The first slice of milestone 2 completes payouts by polling alone, so the system is correct without webhooks and webhooks (2.2) only make it faster (failure mode F18). The PR also added the milestone 2 design.

## What shipped

- **Fake provider:** `POST /payouts` answers `202 pending` and settles later; `GET /payouts/{id}`; `payout_fail` now means "accepted, then failed".
- **Provider client:** new `Accepted` and `Pending` classes, and `GetPayout`, which checks the ID, amount and currency.
- **Waiting and polling.** An accepted payout waits in `AWAITING_CALLBACK` with its `po_…` reference. After the callback deadline, workers claim it for a poll under the usual leases and fencing. Paid means ledger posting and `SUCCEEDED` in one transaction; failed means refund, or parking under the pivot rule; pending means wait again with backoff.
- **Polls never create doubt (D17).** A `GET` moves no money, so polls are recorded as attempts of kind `poll`, and a crashed poll goes back to waiting, not `IN_DOUBT`. The pivot rule now reads only the history of send attempts.
- **Bounded waiting.** A payout that never resolves ends `DEAD` as `callback_unresolved`, with a runbook section; `confirm-step` and `retry-step` both work on it.

## What broke or surprised us

- **`retry-step` after `callback_unresolved` got one poll, then parked again.** The budget counted every poll the step ever had. It is now counted per acceptance.
- **A payout accepted, parked, then settled failed was mislabelled near key retention.** The lookup read "failed" as unknown, burned the retry budget and ended `attempts_exhausted_in_doubt`. A step holding the payout's reference now polls it directly, and a lookup that finds it failed treats that as a refusal.
- **Our polling budget assumed minutes; real payouts take days.** 8 polls (about 15 minutes) would have paged on most bank payouts. The budget is now a total wait since acceptance (`IDEM_CALLBACK_MAX_WAIT`, default 72h), polls back off up to 1h, and a waiting payout no longer counts as stuck until its poll is due.
- **A regression from milestone 1.** A payout that ran out of attempts while rate limited, then failed cleanly after an operator's retry, parked instead of refunding. An attempt that provably moved nothing is now not doubt, even the last one.
- **Chaos found an older bug (seed 56214, which failed the same way on `main`).** A `409 still processing` was retried after a fixed short delay, so a busy provider used up all 8 attempts in about a second. In-flight retries now back off with equal jitter, a minimum of about 4 minutes over 8 attempts. The fake provider's hang (30s against a 1s client timeout) became configurable; chaos uses 3s.
- Smaller fixes: `confirm-step` refuses a reference other than the one the provider gave; `admin list` shows `POLLS`; migration 00008 now rolls back on a database that used async payouts.

## Evidence

- The reviewer confirmed the plan's five risk cases: a crashed poll is not doubt, a never-settling payout ends in a bounded `DEAD`, `confirm-step` accepts the payout's own reference, a lost `202` re-sends and waits on the same payout, and attempt numbers stay unique.
- `make test` (14 packages, `-race`, real Postgres 18), `make lint` 0 issues, `make build-release`, chaos-tagged tests.
- Chaos after all fixes: seeds 56214, 1 and 891769 (90s) passed with 0 violations and about 800 payout polls. Seed 891769 at 60s was judged vacuous (no worker kill met work in flight): the harness being strict, not a code fault.
- Compose smoke: a payout waited in `AWAITING_CALLBACK`, then one poll led to `SUCCEEDED`; `payout_fail` led to `COMPENSATED`; `idem check` 0 violations.
