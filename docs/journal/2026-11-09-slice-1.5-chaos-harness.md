# Slice 1.5 — Chaos harness

*Merged 2026-11-09 · [PR #4](https://github.com/AdamDiouri/idem/pull/4) · [plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-11-09-slice-1.5-chaos-harness.md) · written retrospectively on 2026-11-10 from the PR and commit record*

Unit and flow tests run in one process. This slice runs idem as real processes and tries hard to break it, then checks every invariant. It also adds the pivot safety rule.

## What shipped

- **Pivot safety rule.** A payout refused *after* an ambiguous attempt goes `DEAD` (`permanent_after_in_doubt`) and the workflow is parked for a human, not refunded. A real provider might refuse a retry even though an earlier attempt paid the merchant.
- **New invariants.** I4 (liveness, after quiescence): no workflow unsettled and no step left `RUNNING` or `IN_DOUBT`. I6: one workflow per inbound idempotency key.
- **`make chaos`.** A throwaway Postgres container, the fake provider, the API, 4 workers and 2 sweepers run under a supervisor that restarts crashed children. A seeded plan applies `kill -9`, `SIGSTOP` beyond the lease, API and database restarts, and provider faults. Workers also carry armed `exit` failpoints.
- **Misbehaving clients.** Open-loop traffic from impatient, double-clicking, reformatting and malformed clients, spread over the whole campaign.
- **A verdict that must be earned.** After the campaign everything settles, then I1 to I7 are checked and every accepted workflow ID is cross-checked against the database. Every disruption kind must meet work in flight, and provider faults and failpoints must actually fire.

## What broke or surprised us

- **The first campaigns attacked a mostly idle system (critical).** All traffic arrived in the first half-second, so database and API restarts and provider faults hit nothing, yet the report counted them and passed. An independent reviewer ran its own campaigns and caught it. Traffic is now spread over the campaign, the default load rose from 300 to 1,000 workflows, each event records the work in flight when it fired, and the verdict requires effective events of every kind and real fault hits. The results below are the re-run numbers.
- **The supervisor could undo a stop.** A stop during the 500ms restart window let a worker come back with failpoints armed, and signals aimed at a dead process counted as executed. Both fixed.
- **Failpoints could be silently unarmed.** Failpoint exits are now required and counted, and a `nofailpoint` binary refuses `IDEM_FAILPOINTS`.
- **The harness leaked.** Ctrl-C during `docker run` left a container; every run leaked a data volume; two runs with the same seed could kill each other's database. Fixed with cleanup on any exit, `docker rm -f -v`, and container names unique per run.
- **Other review fixes:** events that failed to apply now fail the run (an already-down target is recorded as skipped), I4 also covers steps, parking is classified by reason, and the API-outage client test provably hits the outage.

## Evidence

Campaigns at 90s, 1,000 workflows each, all with 0 violations:

| Seed | Effective events | Failpoint exits | Outcomes (succeeded / compensated / failed / parked) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 32/32, 1 skipped | 99 | 739 / 74 / 66 / 5 |
| 2 | 17/17, 2 skipped | 104 | 571 / 134 / 179 / 19 |
| 3 | 34/34, 3 skipped | 120 | 748 / 80 / 57 / 6 |

- Every parked workflow was `payout permanent_after_in_doubt`: the pivot rule at work.
- Interrupting a campaign at 0.3s, 0.5s and 20s exited 2 and left no container, process or volume.
- `make test`, `go test -tags nofailpoint ./internal/failpoint/`, `make lint` 0 issues, `make build-release`, and the 30s chaos-tagged test.

## Left open

- `Restarts` does not count sweepers or the fake provider; a fake-provider crash should be a harness error.
- No `Pdeathsig`: if `chaos` itself is SIGKILLed, its children are orphaned.
- Overlapping pauses on one worker can end early; a small `FreePort` race; the integration test has no overall timeout.
- Fenced-commit and `IN_DOUBT` resolution counts are not in the report yet; they arrive with 1.6 metrics.
